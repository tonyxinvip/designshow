Best Before

Every note carries the date it was last checked. Ninety days is the shelf life. Past that the paper yellows, the ink thins, a corner lifts, the pin loses its grip and the note comes off the wall. The date stamp fades slowest of all: long after the writing has gone soft, the date is still legible. Where a note used to hang, the plaster is a shade cleaner and the pin is still in it. The page reads the real calendar, so how much is still up depends on when you open it. Click one to check it again and it goes back up white, dated today.

Kaleidoscope

A kaleidoscope is two mirrors set at an angle. Reflections come in pairs, so the symmetry group is DN, not CN. Drop the mirror step and the figure collapses into a pinwheel. Hard to say what is wrong with it, easy to see that something is.

Life Is an Ocean

A friend’s handwritten letter, in Chinese. Twenty-two lines circle the frame; whichever one reaches the top burns brightest, then spirals inward and falls into the hole at the centre. Every character in the wall behind them is taken from the letter. Change the letter and the wall changes with it. A piano plays it: one phrase per line, so the music is on the animation's clock rather than its own. Change how long a turn takes and the playing changes with it.

Armature

Rods, hoops and ball joints sitting at real depths. Fly through it and the near ones sweep past while the far ones barely move. Measured on the sculpture this came from, that ratio is about twenty to one, and it is the whole effect. A flat zoom cannot fake it. Switch the sound on and one clock drives both: the flight nudges forward on the beat, and every hoop you pass through answers with a note. Nine dials for the music, and not a byte of audio in the page. It is built while you listen.

Oculus

Nothing in this one spins. I measured the footage it came from: the rotation between frames is a median of zero degrees, the zoom is zero pixels. The rings hold dead still, then jump. Everything that reads as motion is that, plus a colour split that grows with the radius and a picture that keeps turning inside out. The dome is built, not drawn: every slat is a box on a spherical cap that runs into a drum coming down at you, so you see the ends and the sides and the near ones are more than twice the size of the far. It repeats exactly twelve times. The light does not, which is what stops it reading as wallpaper. Every slat picks its own colour out of one hue family, and the family itself drifts — wandering inside its own range rather than touring the wheel, because a full lap would leave the family behind. Colour never carries the form. The sound has no beat either, for the same reason as the picture. Each jump rings a bell.

Rosette

Folded paper, tier upon tier, each ring turning against the one inside it. That counter-turn is measured: on the thing this came from the middle runs one way at half a degree per frame and everything outside runs the other, with no zoom at all. Each tier has its own petal count, so each strikes its note at its own interval and the rhythm you hear is the geometry rather than a bar line. Every petal draws its own paper and its own border — nothing here is scanned.
