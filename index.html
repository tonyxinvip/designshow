Kaleidoscope

A kaleidoscope is two mirrors set at an angle. Reflections come in pairs, so the symmetry group is DN, not CN. Drop the mirror step and the figure collapses into a pinwheel. Hard to say what is wrong with it, easy to see that something is.

Armature

Rods, hoops and ball joints sitting at real depths. Fly through it and the near ones sweep past while the far ones barely move. Measured on the sculpture this came from, that ratio is about twenty to one, and it is the whole effect. A flat zoom cannot fake it. Switch the sound on and one clock drives both: the flight nudges forward on the beat, and every hoop you pass through answers with a note. Nine dials for the music, and not a byte of audio in the page. It is built while you listen.

Oculus

Nothing in this one spins. I measured the footage it came from: the rotation between frames is a median of zero degrees, the zoom is zero pixels. The rings hold dead still, then jump. Everything that reads as motion is that, plus a colour split that grows with the radius and a picture that keeps turning inside out. The dome is built, not drawn: every slat is a box on a spherical cap that runs into a drum coming down at you, so you see the ends and the sides and the near ones are more than twice the size of the far. It repeats exactly twelve times. The light does not, which is what stops it reading as wallpaper. Every slat picks its own colour out of one hue family and keeps it through the symmetry, but colour never carries the form. The sound has no beat either, for the same reason as the picture. Each jump rings a bell.
